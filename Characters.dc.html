What's done is done.
A Deva of Japanese Buddhism who is said to be a god of destiny, venerated in the Tendai tradition. He is usually depicted wielding a bow and arrow.

It was said that being unable to see the star at night was an omen of imminent death.

Never again will I be subjugated!

Time spent on frivolities is time stolen from those who came before you.
Patriarch of the Odoriba family, and the head of the family’s shrine. He is deeply steeped in tradition, which in many ways makes him quite rigid and inflexible, especially in dealing with his children who appear to share little interest in priestly duties.
He is especially critical of his son, believing that his focus should be on learning how to run the shrine which he will one day inherit rather than on frivolous things. While Kazumasa would never confront him on this, Etsuko does – and the two of them often butt heads and have a strained relationship.
While he does not wish to hinder his children’s potential, he does want them to lead successful lives and believes that he and his family have already carved out a successful niche for them in the shrine. He would prefer that they focus on this rather than things he considers “frivolous”, and things that he believes could potentially endanger the family’s reputation.

As an old man, he suffers from Alzheimer’s, and as such, it has affected his memory, causing him to drift in and out of the past and present.
